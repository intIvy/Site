Washington Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Washington’s My Health My Data Act treats the body measurements behind a size recommendation as consumer health data, and so do we. This policy is the stand-alone disclosure that Act requires: what we collect, why, who ever sees it, and the rights Washington gives you over it.

Version 1.0 Effective 11 November 2026 Last updated 11 November 2026 Applies to Washington consumers and to consumer health data collected in Washington
The short version
  • What we collect. Height, weight, age, and either a body shape or the garment sizes you already wear — only what you type in, only after you choose to use Ask Ella.
  • Why. To predict your measurements and recommend a size. That is the whole use.
  • Who sees it. Us, and the service providers who run our infrastructure under contract. The shop receives a size, never your measurements.
  • We do not sell it. Ever. A sale would require your separate, signed authorisation, which we will not ask for.
  • You can withdraw consent or delete it. One request, no reason needed, including from backups and from our processors.
  • You can appeal. If we refuse a request, we explain why in writing and you can escalate to the Washington Attorney General.

1. Why this policy exists

Anthrospecs Ltd builds Ask Ella, a size recommendation service for clothing retailers. Our main privacy policy explains everything we do with data, for everyone. Washington’s My Health My Data Act (chapter 19.373 RCW) asks for something more specific: a stand-alone consumer health data privacy policy, linked separately from our homepage, covering the data the Act protects.

The Act defines “consumer health data” broadly enough that bodily measurements may fall within it. Rather than argue the point, we treat the body and fit data behind Ask Ella as consumer health data for Washington consumers. This page is written on that basis.

It applies to you if you are a Washington resident, or if your consumer health data is collected while you are in Washington. Everything here is in addition to the main policy, not instead of it.

2. The consumer health data we collect, and why

We collect nothing until you choose to open Ask Ella and answer its questions. The categories, and the purpose each is used for:

CategoryExamplesPurpose and use
Core metricsHeight, weight, ageInput to the model that predicts your measurements
Body shape, womenswearOne of five shapes you selectRefines the prediction
Known garment sizes, menswearChest or jacket size, trouser waist, inside leg — each optionalCorrects the prediction with sizes you already know fit
Bra size, where askedBand and cupRefines the womenswear prediction
Sizing systemWomen’s or men’sChooses which model sizes you
Your digital twinPredicted measurements — bust or chest, waist, hip, inside leg, neck and othersDerived by us from the figures above; compared against the garment’s size chart to recommend a size
Fit outcomesThe size we recommended, whether you accepted it, and — only where the retailer sends it — whether the item was kept or returnedMeasures whether our recommendations fit, so they improve

Each category is used for the purpose given and for nothing else: no advertising, no profiling beyond the size recommendation itself, no inference of health conditions. Your twin is stored against a random identifier generated on your device, not against your name, email address or shop account — the widget is never given those. Our standing engineering rule is that predicted measurements are never returned to the browser: the panel shows a size, not a list of your measurements.

We collect no photographs, no body scans, and no precise location, and we do not process biometric data as the Act defines it.

3. Where it comes from

  1. You, typing answers into the Ask Ella widget or browser extension.
  2. Us, deriving your digital twin and the recommendation from those answers.
  3. The retailer whose site you are on, where they send us a purchase or return matched to a recommendation.

There is no third source. We do not buy, license or scrape consumer health data from anyone.

4. What we share, and with whom

We share consumer health data only as needed to run the service, and only these categories: the inputs you typed, the derived twin, and fit outcomes. The parties:

Each is a processor acting under a contract that limits them to providing their service to us. The retailer whose site you are on is not on this list for a reason: it receives the recommended size and anonymised, aggregated fit statistics. It never receives your measurements or your answers.

We have no affiliates, and we share consumer health data with no third party for that third party’s own use. You may ask us for a list of everyone we have disclosed your consumer health data to — see section 7.

5. What we never do

  • We do not sell consumer health data. The Act would require your separate, signed authorisation before any sale, valid for one year and revocable at any time. We will not ask for one, because we do not sell data.
  • We do not use it for advertising, and we do not track you across the web.
  • We do not geofence. We operate no geofence around any facility that provides health care services, and we do not use location data at all beyond inferring the country a request comes from.

6. Consent

We collect consumer health data only with your consent, and we treat your deliberate act of opening Ask Ella, typing your figures and asking for a size as that consent — given freely, for the specific purpose this policy describes, before anything is collected. If you close the panel instead, nothing is collected.

We do not collect more than the purpose needs, we do not use what we collect for any purpose you have not consented to, and sharing is limited to the processors in section 4, which the Act permits as necessary to provide the service you asked for. Anything beyond that would require your separate consent, which we would ask for plainly, not bury in an update.

7. Your rights

Under the My Health My Data Act you may:

  • Confirm and access. Ask whether we collect, share or sell consumer health data about you, and receive a copy of it, including a list of all third parties and affiliates with whom we have shared it and an active email address or other online mechanism to contact each one.
  • Withdraw consent. Withdraw your consent to our collection and sharing of your consumer health data, at any time, as easily as you gave it.
  • Delete. Have your consumer health data deleted — from our live systems, from our backups as they cycle, and by every processor holding it, whom we notify. One request, no reason needed.

How to exercise them. Write to privacy@anthrospecs.com. Because we hold no name or email address against your twin, we will need the ella_user_id from the device you used, and we will tell you exactly where to find it — this is to protect you, not to obstruct you. We respond within 45 days, extendable once by a further 45 days where reasonably necessary, in which case we tell you within the first period and explain why. Exercising any right costs nothing and changes nothing about how Ask Ella works for you, except where you have asked us to delete the very data a recommendation needs.

8. Appeals

If we refuse to act on a request, we tell you why in writing. You may appeal by replying to that refusal, or by writing to privacy@anthrospecs.com with “Appeal” in the subject line. A person who was not involved in the original decision reviews it, and within 45 days we send you a written explanation of the outcome and the reasons for it.

If the appeal does not resolve it, you may complain to the Washington Attorney General at atg.wa.gov/file-complaint. The Act is also enforceable by you directly under Washington’s Consumer Protection Act.

9. Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the version and date above change with it, and a material change is flagged in the widget before it applies to you. Earlier versions are available on request.
