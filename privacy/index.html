Privacy Policy

Ask Ella works out your size from a handful of figures you give us. This policy explains exactly what those figures are, what we do with them, who else ever sees them, and how to get them back or have them deleted.

Version 1.0 Effective 12 October 2026 Last updated 12 October 2026 Applies to the UK, the EEA, Switzerland and the United States
The short version
  • What we ask for. Height, weight, age, and either a body shape (womenswear) or the garment sizes you already wear (menswear). Nothing else is needed to size you.
  • What we never ask for. No photographs or body scans, no name, no address, no payment details, no precise location, no health information.
  • What we build. A set of predicted body measurements — your “digital twin” — stored against a random identifier, not against your name.
  • Who sees it. You, and the people at Anthrospecs who have to. The shop you are on receives a size and, separately, anonymised and aggregated fit statistics. It never receives your measurements.
  • We do not sell your data. Not under any definition, including the broad ones in US state law. We do not use it for advertising and we do not track you across the web.
  • You can delete it. One request, no reason needed, and the twin and everything tied to it goes. See your rights.

5. What we never collect

Some of this is worth stating in the negative, because people reasonably assume a sizing tool does it.

  • No photographs, videos, or body scans. Ask Ella does not use your camera. There is no photograph-based measurement step, and we do not accept images of you. If we ever build one, it will be a separate, clearly described feature with its own opt-in consent, and this policy will be updated before it ships.
  • No biometric identifiers. We do not collect fingerprints, face or hand geometry, iris or retina scans, or voiceprints, and we do not use any data we hold to identify you from your body.
  • No health information. We do not ask about medical conditions, pregnancy, disability, medication, or body composition goals, and we ask you not to type any of it into the widget. If you do, we will delete it.
  • No identity or payment data through the widget. No name, email address, postal address, phone number, card details or bank details. Your order and payment stay with the retailer and its payment provider.
  • No precise location. No GPS, no device location permission, no street-level geolocation.
  • No advertising. We run no ad network, place no advertising or social media pixels in the widget, build no advertising profiles, and do not track you across unrelated sites.
  • No sale of your data. We do not sell, rent or licence it, under any definition of “sale”, including the broad ones in California and other US state laws, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

6. Is body data sensitive?

A straight answer, because the law and ordinary intuition do not line up here.

Under the UK and EU GDPR, the figures we collect are personal data but are not, in our assessment, special category data under Article 9. Height, weight, age and garment sizes are not data concerning health: we do not process them to reveal anything about your health, we do not infer any condition, diagnosis or risk from them, and the output is a clothing size. They are not biometric data under Article 4(14) either, because biometric data means data resulting from specific technical processing for the purpose of uniquely identifying a person, and we never process body data to identify anyone.

That is a legal classification, not a judgement about how it feels. Many people consider their weight and their measurements among the most private things about them. So we treat this data as if it were sensitive whether or not the law requires it: we ask for consent before we build a twin rather than relying on legitimate interests; we keep the twin pseudonymous; we never return measurements to the browser; we never pass them to the retailer; we never put them into a third-party AI model; and deletion is honoured without asking you why.

Illinois and Texas. Our processing does not involve “biometric identifiers” as the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act or the Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act define them, for the reasons above. We make this statement deliberately and would obtain separate written consent before any processing that changed it.

Washington State. The My Health My Data Act defines “consumer health data” broadly enough that bodily measurements may fall within it. Rather than argue the point, we apply that Act’s protections to the body data of Washington consumers: we collect it only with your consent, we do not sell it, we would seek a separate signed authorisation before any sale, and you may ask us to delete it under section 15.

8. Consent and storage on your device

Ask Ella writes three things to your browser’s local storage and sets one first-party cookie, all listed in section 4.2. None of them is strictly necessary to display the page you are on, so under the UK PECR and the ePrivacy Directive they require your consent before they are set.

How that consent is obtained depends on where you met the widget:

  • On a retailer’s site, the retailer’s own consent banner is the mechanism. We require every retailer, by contract, to obtain valid consent for Ask Ella’s storage and to honour a refusal. Where consent has not been given, the widget runs without writing the identifiers: you can still get a size, but you will be asked the questions again next time, and we cannot connect that recommendation to a later purchase or return.
  • In the browser extension, installing it and completing setup is the consent, and you can clear everything from the extension’s own settings.

You can withdraw this consent at any time by clearing site data for the shop in your browser, by using the retailer’s consent controls, or by writing to us. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of what we did beforehand.

We set no advertising, analytics or social media cookies of our own, and we do not use fingerprinting or any other technique designed to recognise your device without storage.

9. How the recommendation is made

The size you see is produced automatically. We think you are entitled to know how, both as a matter of fairness and because Article 22 of the UK and EU GDPR gives you rights about automated decisions.

  1. Your twin. A model trained on a research dataset of over eleven thousand anthropometric body scans predicts your key measurements from the figures you gave us. Nobody’s individual scan is identifiable in that dataset, and your own data is not added to it.
  2. The garment. A second step reads the product’s description, fabric composition and the retailer’s size chart to work out the intended fit, how much the fabric gives, and what each size label actually measures.
  3. The comparison. We compare the two, weighting the measurements that govern that kind of garment — the shoulder and chest for a jacket, the waist and seat for trousers, the bust for a top — and name the size with the best fit, with a sentence explaining the trade-off. Where we cannot do this with confidence, we say so and recommend nothing rather than guess.

This is a suggestion about clothing. It has no legal effect on you and we do not consider it a decision producing legal or similarly significant effects, so the Article 22 prohibition does not apply. Even so: no human reviews each recommendation before you see it, you are free to ignore it, and if you want a person to look at a recommendation you think is wrong, write to us and one will.

Our model development uses a third-party large language model to interpret product descriptions and size charts. Your body data is never sent to it. Only garment text goes out, and the provider is contractually barred from training on what we send.

13. Security

  • In transit. Everything travels over TLS 1.2 or better. On Shopify, requests from the storefront reach us through Shopify’s app proxy and are signed, so we can verify they came from the shop they claim to.
  • At rest. Encrypted with AES-256.
  • Pseudonymisation. Your twin is keyed to a random identifier. We hold no name or email address against it.
  • Need-to-know access. Access to systems holding body data is restricted to the people whose work requires it, protected by multi-factor authentication, logged, and reviewed. Row-level security separates one retailer’s data from another’s.
  • Data minimisation by design. Measurements are never sent to the browser, never sent to the retailer, and never sent to a third-party AI model.
  • Suppliers. We assess each processor’s security before we use them and bind them contractually to equivalent standards.
  • Breaches. We have an incident response procedure. Where a personal data breach is likely to risk your rights and freedoms we notify the ICO within 72 hours and, where the risk is high, we notify you directly and without undue delay. Where we are a processor, we notify the retailer without undue delay so they can meet their own duties.

No system is perfectly secure, and we will not claim otherwise. What we can say is that we collect as little as the job allows, keep it for as short a time as is useful, and keep it separable from your identity.

14. Your rights (UK, EEA, Switzerland)

Where we are the controller, you have the following rights. They are free to exercise and you do not need to give a reason.

  • Access — a copy of the personal data we hold about you, including your predicted measurements, and information about how we use it.
  • Rectification — correction of anything inaccurate. In practice the fastest route is to reopen the widget and change your answers, which rebuilds your twin.
  • Erasure — deletion of your twin and the data behind it.
  • Restriction — a pause on our processing while a dispute about accuracy or legitimate interests is resolved.
  • Objection — to any processing we base on legitimate interests, including our analytics and model improvement.
  • Portability — your data in a structured, machine-readable format, or sent directly to another provider where that is technically feasible.
  • Withdraw consent — at any time, for anything we do on the basis of consent.
  • Automated decisions — the information in section 9, and a human to look at a recommendation you think is wrong.
  • Complain — to a supervisory authority, as set out below.

How to exercise them. Write to privacy@anthrospecs.com. Because we hold no name or email address against your twin, we will need the ella_user_id from the device you used, and we will tell you exactly where to find it — this is to protect you, not to obstruct you. We respond within one month, and may extend that by two further months for a complex request, in which case we will tell you within the first month and explain why. If we cannot identify your data from what you can give us, we will say so plainly rather than refuse without explanation.

16. Children

Ask Ella is not for children. It is intended for people aged 16 and over, we do not knowingly collect data from anyone younger, and we do not offer children’s sizing. If you are under 16, please do not use the widget.

If we learn that we hold data from a child, we delete it. If you believe a child has used Ask Ella, write to privacy@anthrospecs.com and we will remove it and confirm that we have. For US residents under 13, we comply with the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act; for the UK, we have regard to the ICO’s Age Appropriate Design Code in how the widget is built.
